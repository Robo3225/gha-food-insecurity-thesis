5  Results
This chapter reports the results of the study in the order of its contributions: the composition of the prediction target and the evaluation rows (Section 5.1); the outcome of the pre-registered model-selection gate (Section 5.2); the benchmark of the two gradient-boosting models against persistence and the FEWS NET projection (Section 5.3); the added value of biophysical signal beyond recent phase history, measured by ablation and by feature attribution (Section 5.4); predictive uncertainty (Section 5.5); and transferability to Somalia (Section 5.6). Section 5.7 summarises the results. Their interpretation is reserved for Chapter 6.
Several conventions apply throughout. Validation windows are identified by the calendar year of the period being predicted (t + h), not of the feature time: 2021 is the window of drought onset, 2022 the crisis peak, and 2023 the post-peak year in which the rains returned but crisis prevalence remained elevated. The three-month horizon (h = 3) is the primary horizon; the one-month horizon (h = 1) is reported as the floor at which persistence dominates. The primary metric is the area under the precision–recall curve (PR-AUC), reported with the area under the ROC curve (ROC-AUC) and the Brier score (Section 4.8). Results are reported per window rather than pooled, and a row count accompanies every score.
Each method is scored on the rows for which it can issue a forecast and the target is observed. The models can forecast every row with an available target; persistence additionally requires the phase at feature time; and the FEWS NET projection exists only on the smaller subset of rows with a period-matched projection, and only at h = 3 (Table 5.3). Every comparison between methods is row-matched: when a model is compared with a baseline, the model is re-scored on exactly the baseline's rows, and the row count of each comparison is reported (Section 4.5). Where a model's score on its own full row-set is reported alongside, its row count is given separately.

5.2  Model selection
The pre-registered model-selection gate (Section 4.7) evaluated an attention-based Temporal Fusion Transformer against three criteria fixed before results were seen: stable training at full panel scale; discrimination within 0.05 PR-AUC of the gradient-boosting benchmark on identical evaluation rows; and coverage between 70% and 90% for the nominal 80% predictive interval. The gate was assessed at h = 3, the horizon at which the forecasting problem is non-trivial. The candidate was scored with a continuous probability of Phase 3+ derived from its own predictive quantiles, the like-for-like counterpart of the benchmark models' probabilities; scoring it with a thresholded point prediction would have understated it. Its specification is given in Appendix A. Table 5.4 reports the outcome.

5.5.2  Confidence tiers
Each forecast is assigned a HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW confidence tier from two inputs: the width of its calibrated interval, and a distribution-shift score measuring how far its features lie from the training distribution. Forecasts made under detected shift are downgraded (Section 4.10). The tiers are designed to stratify the reliability of forecasts. Whether they do so on the real predictions was not evaluated in this study, and this section makes no empirical claim that they do; the validation is set out as future work in Section 7.5.
5.6  Transferability to Somalia
Models trained on Kenya and Ethiopia were applied to Somalia, which contributed no training rows; Somalia's features are constructed within Somalia (Section 4.11). Results are reported per window on Somalia's rows, against Somalia's own persistence and FEWS NET baselines. Absolute scores under transfer are not directly comparable with the in-pool scores of Section 5.3, which are computed on rows from all three countries, so comparisons between the two settings are made on increments within each setting, such as B − C, rather than on absolute levels. Prevalence on Somalia's persistence rows was 0.279 in 2021, 0.690 in 2022, and 0.666 in 2023: the later windows are majority-crisis, which raises the no-skill reference for PR-AUC. As Section 5.1 showed, half of Somalia's persistence rows in 2022 and 2023 are excluded by the two missing assessment rounds.

This is the only comparison at h = 3 in the study in which a gradient-boosting model scored below a baseline on identical rows. On the same rows, the FEWS NET projection exceeded Somalia's persistence baseline in 2021 (by 0.053) and 2022 (by 0.083), and the two were effectively tied in 2023 (persistence higher by 0.005). Every comparison with the FEWS NET projection in 2022 and 2023 rests on one assessment round of 198 units.

Appendix A — Specification of the attention-based candidate
[Appendix block, supporting Section 5.2.]
The attention-based candidate evaluated in the model-selection gate is a Temporal Fusion Transformer (Lim et al., 2021), implemented with the pytorch-forecasting library, version 1.8.0 (with PyTorch 2.13.0 and Lightning 2.6.5). Table A.1 gives its specification.
Table A.1 — Specification of the attention-based candidate.
Element	Setting
Target	Ordinal IPC phase (1–5) at t + h
Horizon evaluated	h = 3
Encoder length	6 months, fixed in advance
Loss	Quantile loss at the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles
Scoring for PR-AUC	Continuous probability of Phase 3+, P(phase ≥ 3) = 1 − F(3), where F is a piecewise-linear predictive distribution through the three predicted quantiles, held flat beyond them
Hidden size	32
Attention heads	2
Dropout	0.2
Learning rate	0.03 (Adam optimiser)
Training	Maximum 30 epochs; gradient clipping at 0.1; early stopping on training loss with patience 5; random seed 42
Normalisation	Target normalised by reporting unit
Static categorical inputs	Climate class and country
Missing predictor values	Forward-filled within unit, with a fill indicator
Evaluation rows	Identical to those of the gradient-boosting benchmark (Section 5.2)
